    Part 2: API & data source plan

    No single source sees all AI usage. Vendor APIs are precise but only cover tools we already pay for. Shadow AI shows up somewhere else: in expense reports, SSO and OAuth logs, and network traffic. The plan is to combine four kinds of signal and join them on employee email, with the HRIS as the source of truth for people.

    1 · Collect

    Vendor admin APIs, IdP logs, expense/card feeds, HRIS, network/CASB.

    2 · Normalize

    Map merchants and domains to a catalog of AI tools; de-duplicate people by email; attach department from HRIS.

    3 · Compute

    MTD spend, run-rate forecast, % of limit, seat utilization, approved vs. unapproved.

    4 · Act

    Alerts to Slack/email, seat reclaim, API spend caps, approval workflow for new tools.

    Can we get consumption data from ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and Midjourney?

    Mostly yes, but only for company-managed accounts. The answer depends on how the tool is bought:

    • Metered APIs (OpenAI API, Anthropic API): yes, precisely. Both offer admin usage and cost endpoints that return tokens and dollars by model, project/workspace and API key. They need an org admin key.
    • Seat products (ChatGPT Enterprise, Claude Team/Enterprise, GitHub Copilot, M365 Copilot, Gemini): partly. We can get seat assignments, active users and last-activity dates, so utilization is available. Cost per seat comes from the contract, not the API. How much detail you get depends on the plan; most of it requires Enterprise tiers.
    • Midjourney and any personal account (ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro, etc.): no vendor API. These are the shadow-AI cases, and they're exactly what vendor APIs can't see. We find them through expense/card data (spend), SSO/OAuth grants (who signed in), and CASB or a browser extension (free-tier use).

    Endpoint names and plan requirements change often. Confirm each against the vendor's current docs during scoping.

    Sequencing

    1. MVP (≈ 4–6 weeks): SSO + expense/card + HRIS + OpenAI/Anthropic cost APIs + limits entered by Finance. This covers sanctioned spend, utilization and most paid shadow AI.
    2. Phase 2: Copilot / M365 / Gemini usage APIs for deeper utilization, OAuth-grant risk scoring, ERP reconciliation.
    3. Phase 3: CASB / browser-extension discovery for free-tier use; push enforcement (API spend caps, auto-reclaim of inactive seats).

    Open questions and risks

    • Privacy: collect metadata (who, which tool, how much), never prompt content. This needs sign-off from legal and works councils in the EU.
    • Identity matching: personal emails on expense reports and shared API keys break the email join. We need a key-to-owner mapping and fuzzy merchant matching.
    • Plan-gated APIs: the best usage data needs Enterprise tiers. Smaller customers will rely more on SSO and expense data.
    • Freshness mismatch: APIs are hourly, M365 reports lag 1–2 days, expenses lag by weeks. Show "synced X ago" on every number (as the inventory table does).
    • Accrual vs. invoice: the CFO reconciles to invoices. Forecasts are accrual-based and need to be labeled that way.

    Part 3: If I could only show three charts

    A CFO or IT/security lead has three questions: Are we over a limit? Will we be by month end? Who is using what, and is it approved? Each chart answers one of them and can be read in under ten seconds. The KPI tiles and the "Needs attention" list aren't charts. They're the headline and the to-do list.

    1

    Spend vs. limit, by tool (bullet chart)

    One row per tool: solid bar = spent so far, lighter bar = projected rest of month, black tick = limit. Sorted by risk, with unapproved tools grouped at the bottom.

    Question it answers
    Which tools are over, or about to go over, their limit?
    Why this one
    It's the most direct picture of "spend limits". Actual, forecast and limit sit on one line, so you don't have to do math. Putting shadow tools on the same axis with no limit tick makes shadow AI a budget problem the CFO feels, not just a security issue.
    Who acts on it
    CFO / FP&A, and the owner of each tool.
    Rejected alternative
    Pie or donut of spend by tool. It shows share of spend but not position against the limit, which is the actual question.
    2

    Cumulative spend vs. budget, with forecast (burn-up line)

    Actual cumulative spend to today, a dashed forecast to month end, the budget line, and a dotted even-pace guide.

    Question it answers
    Will we stay inside the budget this month, and when would we cross it?
    Why this one
    Limits are bounded in time. A month-to-date number on its own can mislead: "40% spent on day 13" sounds fine until you see that metered API usage is accelerating. The slope against the even-pace line shows that right away and tells you when to step in.
    Who acts on it
    CFO / FP&A, and department heads when filtered.
    Rejected alternative
    Monthly spend bars. They're lagging and only tell you after the money is spent.
    3

    Department × tool adoption (heatmap)

    Rows are departments and columns are tools, split into approved and unapproved. Each cell shows active users, shaded by share of that department's headcount.

    Question it answers
    Who is using what, and where is shadow AI concentrated?
    Why this one
    It covers the "usage" half of the brief in one view. Security spots risky clusters (Sales on Otter.ai recording customer calls). Finance spots duplication (personal ChatGPT Plus next to paid Enterprise seats). Enablement spots gaps (low adoption in Legal). Normalizing by headcount keeps small teams visible.
    Who acts on it
    IT / security lead, plus Finance for consolidation.
    Rejected alternative
    Active users over time. Nice to know, but it doesn't say which team or which tool to act on.

    What I deliberately left out

    • Token counts: meaningless to a CFO. Translate to dollars.
    • Per-employee leaderboards: they feel like surveillance and hurt adoption. Department level is enough to act on.
    • Opaque "risk scores": a number without the reason behind it can't be acted on. The heatmap and the alert text explain the risk instead.
    • Dual-axis spend + users charts: they invite false correlations. Spend and usage stay on separate charts.
